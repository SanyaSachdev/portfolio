Project · CityMall

Next-Day Delivery

Cutting average delivery time from 4.6 to 1.7 days, and making the faster option pay for itself.

Role Product manager
Timeline About 4 months
Worked with Engineering, design, warehouse, last-mile ops
Headline result 4.6 → 1.7 days average delivery

Context

CityMall is an e-commerce grocery platform for customers in India's tier-3 cities, with 1.2 million daily active users, about 50,000 orders a day and INR 100 crore (about USD 11 million) in monthly sales. Delivery dates shown at checkout were typically four to five days out. Customers here plan their household rations around that date, so speed is a planning input more than a convenience.

Problem

Orders took an average of 4.6 days to arrive. For customers who plan their groceries around the delivery date, that wait was the weakest part of the experience. The business question alongside it: could a faster option add to margin instead of costing it?

My role

I drove the launch end to end, aligning engineering, design, warehouse and last-mile operations teams. That covered the checkout experience, pricing, customer research, segmentation, last-mile operations, and the demand forecast and quota logic that decide how much next-day capacity to offer.

Process

01

Testing the price

  • Launched to a 2% user base at three prices: ₹5, ₹9 and ₹12.
  • No promotion anywhere in the app, so take-up reflected natural demand.
  • 26% chose it at ₹5 and 16% at ₹9. Take-up fell away at ₹12, so ₹9 was chosen.
02

An honest checkout

  • One added step just before payment: standard or next-day delivery.
  • Exact dates and times shown, based on order cut-offs.
  • Standard delivery is the default. Nothing is pre-selected.
03

Deciding which customers to charge

  • The new option caused a small drop in conversion.
  • Segment analysis asked where the lost sales outweighed the margin gained.
  • Margin-positive customers were not worth that risk. Customers who buy mainly on heavy discounts were a fair place to earn a fee.
04

Giving the best customers speed by default

  • Reserved delivery capacity for margin-positive customers, so they get a better date without paying.
  • Their next-day share rose to 58%, and almost 80% now get delivery within two days.
05

Order forecast and quota engine

  • Built an order prediction model to estimate how much capacity to hold back each day.
  • Built a quota engine that raises or lowers the reserved share as orders come in.
06

Fixing every step of the chain

  • Next-day orders came in on top of planned capacity, which at times had to double.
  • Added order cut-offs, priority packing in the warehouse and a priority tag in the delivery partner app.
  • Set the refund rules: the fee is refunded on a missed promise, but not when the customer confirms a reschedule with an OTP. A delivery partner who lets an order slip loses the incentive and must deliver it the next day.

Outcome

63% Faster delivery: average time down from 4.6 to 1.7 days
92% Next-day orders delivered on time
+0.28% Margin after discount, from the paid next-day option
58% Of margin-positive customers now get next-day, up from 21%

What I learned

01

Verify a hypothesis before acting on it

  • When conversion dipped, I had an explanation ready.
  • Customer calls and the numbers showed it was wrong.
02

Expect to iterate

  • The quota and capacity system took many rounds to get right.
  • The first version missed its target; later rounds reached it.

Delivery Partner Payout Platform

Paying 3,000+ delivery partners the same day instead of 15 days later, through a payout system built on tags, plans and priorities.

Role Product manager
Timeline About 5 months
Worked with Engineering, design, last-mile operations
Headline result Settlements 15 days → same day

Context

CityMall was replacing commission-based delivery through local shopkeepers with its own hubs and riders. More than 3,000 delivery partners now had to be paid directly, but payouts were calculated manually, prone to errors, and took up to 15 days to arrive.

Problem

High delivery partner churn and slow new-partner acquisition. The drivers were an unpredictable payout cycle, low transparency on per-trip pay and incentives, and a payout structure with no flexibility. The visible symptom was 400 to 500 payout escalations a day.

My role

Designing an automated, tech-led, flexible payout system: its unit metrics (per-trip pay, weight- and distance-based pricing, surge, slabs, daily and weekly incentives), logic and data flow, signed off by leadership. Then redesigning the delivery partner app to make earnings clear.

Process

01

A payout system built on tags, plans and priorities

  • Partners carry tags; plans allow or disallow tags.
  • Priority decides when a partner fits two plans.
  • Calculated in real time, so changing a plan needs no new build.
02

Redesigning the payout unit metrics

  • Per-trip pricing by weight and distance.
  • Multi-slab targets, surge, and daily and weekly incentives.
03

Rebuilding the delivery partner app

  • Built from interviews and field visits with partners.
  • Shows earnings per order and performance.
04

Onboarding and fraud controls

  • Before: one phone number, one ID. People re-registered to claim joining incentives or to walk away from unsettled cash.
  • Now: phone and Aadhaar are checked against every past partner before a new ID is created, with mandatory KYC and selfie verification.
  • Incentive fraud down 20%; INR 4 lakh a month saved.

AI-Led Operations Automation

Three repetitive operations workflows handed to AI, saving INR 3.5 lakh a month across a 19-member team.

Role Product manager
Timeline About 2 months
Worked with Operations, finance, a voice-AI vendor
Headline result INR 3.5 lakh saved a month

Context

The 19-member last-mile operations team carried a high share of manual, repeatable work: bill validation at cash reconciliation, delivery verification calls, and daily capacity planning for 300+ hubs.

Problem

High manual effort on rule-based workflows, leading to slow turnaround, operating cost that scaled with order volume, and inconsistent decisions.

My role

Identified the automation candidates, built the bill validator, defined the voice bot’s conversation design and in-app trigger, and automated capacity planning.

Automations

01

Bill validation for cash reconciliation (OCR + LLM)

  • Standardised bill template, so OCR extracts fixed fields: name, amount, serial number.
  • Validation rules derived from 500+ labelled bills: amount and serial-number match, with an image-quality threshold.
  • LLM (Gemini) as a second-pass check, giving two-step verification.
  • Impact: 86% flagging accuracy and 6 manual hours saved per day.
02

Voice bot for delivery verification

  • Trigger: on a high-value order where the customer declines to share the OTP, the delivery partner raises a request in the app.
  • Flow: the voice bot calls the customer to confirm, and on confirmation the OTP is auto-populated.
  • Conversation design: documented edge cases and expected bot responses; delivered with a third-party vendor.
  • Impact: 80% of manual verification calls automated.
03

Automated daily capacity planning

  • Removed the manual hand-off: delivery partners self-report availability through a gigs feature.
  • Capacity per hub is computed from availability and historical delivery rates (Apps Script).
  • Impact: automated planning across 300+ hubs, replacing manual estimates.

Outcome

INR 3.5 lakh Saved in operations cost every month
3 Repetitive workflows automated
6 hours Of manual bill checking saved a day
80% Of verification calls automated
300+ Hubs with automated capacity planning
